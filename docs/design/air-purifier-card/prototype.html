Air purifier cards

Three directions for the Levoit air-purifier Device and its seven Entities (power, mode, manual fan level, display, child lock, PM2.5, filter life). Data is synthetic. Controls run a simulated Command: about a second pending, then the optimistic Observation the Adapter publishes. The state presets apply to the Living room purifier; Bedroom stays ready for comparison.

What to decide

  • A, B, or C, or a mix. A leads with Power, like the light composite. B leads with the air reading and puts the controls beside it once the card has at least 500px of content width. C is a ToggleTile for grids of quick switches: a press toggles Power, with no mode or fan controls.
  • Mode and fan control. Segmented shows every choice and takes one press. Menus are smaller but take two presses. The stepper keeps Mode segmented and changes the fan one level at a time. In Auto and Sleep, the stored manual level gets a dashed outline, because it isn't measured fan speed. Picking a level switches to Manual, which is how the Adapter behaves.
  • Air category tone. You chose words plus tone: Good has a green dot, Moderate and Unhealthy for sensitive groups use warn, and Unhealthy or worse uses error. The bands are EPA's 2024 24-hour outdoor breakpoints applied to an instant indoor reading, which is an approximation; Source should say so. The category sits beside PM2.5 rather than in the Status slot, which stays for Entity status.
  • One shared status line per card. Each light control reserves its own line. With five purifier controls that would add about 105px of mostly empty space, so each card reserves a single line that names the control (“Switching to Sleep…”). This differs from DESIGN.md and needs your decision.
  • Lag after a poll. The Rolled back preset shows the documented VeSync lag: a power-off was accepted, then the next poll reported On again. Showing that note needs the card to read the retained Command outcome next to State. Cards don't do that today. If we skip it, the card silently flips back to On.
  • Afterglow. Only a new PM2.5 or filter reading lights the afterglow. Optimistic control changes don't glow, so the glow never reads as a Command result.
